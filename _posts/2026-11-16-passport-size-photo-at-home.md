Paying studio fees every time you need a passport-size photo for a visa, exam admit card, or government ID is unnecessary when any modern smartphone camera can take a sharper portrait at home. The difference between a homemade photo that gets accepted on the first try and one that gets rejected comes down to four rules: flat daylight without wall shadows, standing half a meter away from a plain wall, framing your head to 70–80% of the height, and cropping to the exact millimeter or inch size required by your official portal.

2. **Stand 40–50 cm in front of a plain white or off-white wall** facing a window in daytime so no shadow falls behind your ears.

## Common Official Passport Photo Sizes (Always Check Your Official Portal)

Before cropping your photo, verify which dimension standard your specific application requires. Using a `35 × 45 mm` rectangle on a portal that requires a `2 × 2 inch` square is one of the most common reasons for rejection:

| Standard Size | Millimeters / Inches | Pixel Equivalent at 300 DPI | Common Official Uses (Verify Current Rules) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2 × 2 inches (Square)** | `51 × 51 mm` (`2 × 2 in`) | `600 × 600 px` | **US Department of State** passports & visas; **Indian Passport Seva / OCI** (ICAO compliant) (Last checked: November 2026 [VERIFY]) |
| **35 × 45 mm (Portrait)** | `35 × 45 mm` (`1.38 × 1.77 in`) | `413 × 531 px` | **UK HM Passport Office**, **Schengen / EU visas**, **Australia**, and many Indian exam/PAN forms (Last checked: November 2026 [VERIFY]) |

## Step 1: Lighting, Background, Posture, and Glasses Rules

Automated biometric checks (based on **ICAO Doc 9303** standards) scan your photo for uniform lighting, eye visibility, and background contrast. Follow this setup at home:

1. **Choose a plain white or light off-white wall** during daytime, facing a window with indirect natural light. Turn off yellow overhead ceiling bulbs that cast dark shadows under your eyebrows and chin.
2. **Stand 40 to 50 cm (about 1.5 feet) forward from the wall.** Standing with your back touching the wall casts a dark halo shadow around your head and shoulders, which triggers automated rejection.
3. **Ask a friend to take the photo with the rear camera from 1.5 meters away using `1.5x` or `2x` zoom** (or set your phone on a shelf/tripod with a 3-second timer). Never hold the phone at arm's length with the wide-angle selfie camera—close-up wide lenses make your nose look larger and hide your ears.
4. **Remove eyeglasses and hats:** The US State Department, Indian Passport Seva, and most visa authorities strictly prohibit eyeglasses in new passport photos because frame rims hide the eyes and lenses create glare spots (Last checked: November 2026 [VERIFY]). Head coverings are permitted only for religious or medical reasons, provided the full oval of the face from the bottom of the chin to the top of the forehead is clearly visible.
5. **Keep a neutral expression:** Look straight into the camera lens with your head centered, shoulders square, mouth closed, and both eyes wide open.

## Step 2: Frame and Crop to the Exact Passport Ratio

Once you have a sharp, shadow-free original photo, crop it so your head proportion matches biometric guidelines:

### Method 1: Crop Manually in Your Desktop Image Editor

1. **Open a copy of your portrait** in macOS Preview, Windows Photos, or GIMP. If your iPhone saved the photo as `.heic`, first [convert the HEIC photo to JPG]({{ '/guides/heic-to-jpg-iphone-photos/' | relative_url }}).
2. **Set the crop aspect ratio** to `1 : 1` (for a `2 × 2 inch` photo) or `7 : 9` (for a `35 × 45 mm` photo).
3. **Position the crop box** so the distance from the bottom of your chin to the crown of your head takes up **70% to 80%** of the total vertical height (`32 mm to 36 mm` on a `45 mm` tall photo, or `1 inch to 1.375 inches` on a `2 × 2 inch` photo). Leave a clear margin of white background above your hair.
4. **Resize to 300 DPI print pixels** (`600 × 600 px` for `2 × 2 in`, or `413 × 531 px` for `35 × 45 mm`) and export as a high-quality `.jpg`. If an online portal also enforces a strict kilobyte range (such as `20 KB to 50 KB`), follow our guide to [resize a photo and signature to an exact KB and pixel size]({{ '/guides/resize-photo-signature-exact-kb/' | relative_url }}).

## Step 3: How to Print Several Passport Photos on One 4×6 Sheet

Printing a single `35 × 45 mm` image directly on a `4 × 6 inch` (`10 × 15 cm`) photo printer stretches that one tiny face across the entire postcard. To print correctly:

1. **Generate or create a `4 × 6 inch` (`1200 × 1800` pixels at 300 DPI) collage sheet** that tiles 6 copies (for `2 × 2 inch` photos) or 8 copies (for `35 × 45 mm` photos) with thin gray cutting lines between them.
2. **Save the sheet as a standard `.jpg` file** at 95–100% quality.
3. **Print on glossy or matte photo paper** at `100% / Actual Size` (turn off *"Fit to frame"* or *"Borderless crop stretch"* in your printer dialog so the millimeter dimensions stay exact).
4. **Cut neatly inside the gray guide lines** using sharp scissors or a paper trimmer.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Using "Beauty Mode" or skin-smoothing filters:** Smartphone portrait enhancers smooth away moles, freckles, and natural skin texture or alter jawline geometry, which biometric checks flag as digitally altered.
- **Wearing a white shirt against a white wall:** A plain white t-shirt blends into a white background, making your head look like it is floating. Wear a medium- or dark-colored collared shirt or top for clear shoulder contrast.
- **Tilting your chin up or down:** Keep the camera lens at exact eye level so neither your nostrils nor the top of your head are exaggerated.

## If It Still Doesn't Work

- **The online portal says "Background is not plain white":** Even a white wall can look beige or light gray in indoor lighting. Move closer to natural daylight (while staying 40 cm forward from the wall) and increase exposure slightly before cropping.
- **Printed photos came out 3 mm smaller or larger than expected:** Your printer dialog had **Scale to Fit** or **Borderless Expansion** enabled. Re-print with scaling set to **100% (Actual Size)** on a `4 × 6 inch` canvas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I take a passport photo with my phone's front selfie camera?
You should avoid the front selfie camera whenever possible. Front cameras use a wide-angle lens (`~24 mm` equivalent) designed for arm's-length shots, which distorts facial proportions. Having someone use the rear camera from 1.5 meters away at `2x` zoom produces natural, studio-flat proportions.

### Are eyeglasses allowed in passport photos in 2026?
No—most major passport authorities (including the US State Department and Indian Passport Seva) prohibit eyeglasses in passport photos to prevent lens glare and frame shadows over the eyes (Last checked: November 2026 [VERIFY]).

### Should I print passport photos on glossy or matte paper?
Unless your specific visa instruction sheet explicitly asks for matte paper, high-quality **photo-quality glossy or semi-gloss (lustre) paper** is accepted almost everywhere. Never print passport photos on thin plain office printer paper.
